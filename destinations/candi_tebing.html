Candi Tebing Tegallinggah

History & Philosophy

Tegallinggah Cliff Temple is a clear testament to the architectural genius of Balinese ancestors in utilizing the natural landscape. Located on the banks of the Pakerisan River, this site was carved directly into the steep cliff face. The temple is estimated to date from the 12th century and served as a hermitage for monks or sages in the past.

Unlike temples built from stacked stones, this structure is "monolithic." There are facing niches for meditation, symbolizing the concept of duality (Rwa Bhineda) that merges with the flow of the sacred river below.

Destination Analysis (4A)

Attraction

  • Archaeological: A monolithic temple carved directly into the cliff.
  • Natural: Located in a beautiful and tranquil river valley.

Accessibility

  • Route: Passes through the residential area of ​​Tegallinggah Village.
  • Challenge: Must descend hundreds of steps to reach the valley floor.

Amenity

  • Minimalist: Toilets are available in the upper parking area.
  • Facilities: Open meditation areas around the site.

Ancillary

  • Management: Collaboration between the local community and the Department of Culture.
  • Spiritual: Often used for Melukat rituals (self-cleansing).

Preparation Before Coming Here

  • Prepare yourself physically to climb and descend the numerous steps.
  • Bring mosquito repellent as the location is in a humid river valley.
  • Respect the sacred area; climbing onto the temple structures is prohibited.

Looking for Inner Peace?

Tebing Tegallinggah Temple is the perfect destination for those looking to escape the hustle and bustle of modernity.
